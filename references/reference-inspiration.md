## Extracted Principles

## Product Discovery

- Product-heavy stores need fast browsing: search, category tabs, popular sections, and compact product cards.
- Mobile users should see useful products before long SEO/content blocks.
- "For you", "popular", "hot-selling", and category groupings are useful patterns when backed by real data or editorial selection.

## Conversion

- Buy/top-up actions should be visually consistent and easy to tap.
- Discount badges work best when small and close to the relevant product.
- Trust cues should sit near purchase moments, not only in a footer.
- Pricing/value text should use tabular numbers and strong hierarchy.

## Navigation

- Search should be prominent in commerce sections.
- Categories should be scannable and short.
- On mobile, horizontal filters and compact tiles beat huge menus.
- Keep account/cart/wallet actions visually distinct from product categories.

## Visual Quality

- Use clear product imagery, product marks, or consistent reserved media areas.
- Avoid overcrowding cards with too many badges.
- Prefer white surfaces, thin borders, subtle blue highlights, and high-contrast text.
- Let one element carry emphasis: badge, CTA, active state, or image, not all at once.
- Borrow framework-level discipline: base classes plus variants, consistent disabled/focus/hover states, and semantic tokens.
- Do not copy Preline/Tailwind classes, dark-mode variants, scripts, CDN dependencies, or framework-specific attributes into Elementor output.

## Trust and Support

- Useful trust messages: secure payment, instant delivery, verified/authentic products, support availability.
- Keep trust claims honest and generic unless the user supplies proof.
- Do not invent ratings, counts, reviews, partners, certifications, or guarantees.
